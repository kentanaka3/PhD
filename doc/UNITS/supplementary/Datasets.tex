\section*{Annual Seismic Network Evolution and Station Availability
(2005--2025)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Annual Seismic Network Evolution and
Station Availability (2005--2025)}
\label{sec:supp_datasets}
\label{sec:supp_annual_ogs_network}

This supplementary section presents the multi-decadal historical evolution of
the regional seismological monitoring infrastructure operated and curated by
\ac{OGS} \acf{CRS} in collaboration with partnering Italian and international
seismological observatories. In alignment with the central dissertation
objective of evaluating \textbf{+20 years} of continuous observational data
(\textbf{January 1st, 2005 to December 31st, 2025}), the annual diagnostic
pairs compiled below document regional network geometry and telemetry
continuity across 21 consecutive calendar years (2005--2025).

For each calendar year, two complementary diagnostic perspectives are provided:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Station Network Geometry (Left)}: Geographic distribution of
    all permanent and temporary seismological and accelerometric recording
    stations active during the calendar year. The red polygon denotes the
    official \ac{OGS} operational bulletin area, while the blue rectangle
    marks the regional bounding box queried across participating networks
    \eg{\ac{OGS} Friuli network \texttt{OX}, \ac{INGV} National Seismic
      Network \texttt{IV}, Slovenian Environment Agency \texttt{SL},
      GeoSphere Austria \texttt{OE}, Swiss Seismological Service \texttt{CH},
      Autonomous Provinces of Trento \texttt{ST} and Bolzano \texttt{SI},
      MedNet \texttt{MN}, and Italian Strong Motion Network \texttt{IT}
    }.
  \item \textbf{Daily Telemetry Availability (Right)}: Stacked time series of
    active daily station counts partitioned by contributing seismic network
    across all 365 (or 366) days of the year. This diagnostic tracks continuous
    recording continuity, winter telemetry drops, transient station outages,
    and multi-client \ac{FDSN} data ingestion milestones.
\end{enumerate}

All station metadata and geographic coordinates are parsed directly from
official FDSN StationXML inventories using \texttt{ogsutils.inventory}. Daily
recording continuity and trace volumes are audited from continuous miniSEED
archive files using \texttt{ogsutils.waveforms}.

The 21-year monitoring history is presented in seven chronological triennial
diagnostic panels (three calendar years per figure), organized into three
operational epochs:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Early Regional Monitoring Baseline (2005--2010)}: Documented in
    Figures~\ref{fig:supp_ogs_2005_2007} and \ref{fig:supp_ogs_2008_2010}.
  \item \textbf{Transboundary Integration and Network Densification
    (2011--2019)}: Documented in Figures~\ref{fig:supp_ogs_2011_2013},
    \ref{fig:supp_ogs_2014_2016}, and \ref{fig:supp_ogs_2017_2019}.
  \item \textbf{Modern High-Density Multi-Agency Architecture (2020--2025)}:
    Documented in Figures~\ref{fig:supp_ogs_2020_2022} and
    \ref{fig:supp_ogs_2023_2025}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Early Regional Monitoring Baseline (2005--2010)}
During this initial operational baseline, the monitoring network comprised
approximately 15 to 35 active stations concentrated primarily along the Friuli
Prealpine belt, supported by early bilateral data exchange with INGV and
Slovenian networks.

\subsection*{Transboundary Integration and Network Densification (2011--2019)}
This operational period marks the progressive expansion and densification of
the regional network, characterized by the systematic integration of the
\ac{OGS} Friuli network (\texttt{OX}), expanded cross-border telemetry with
Austria (\texttt{OE}) and Slovenia (\texttt{SL}), and the deployment of new
broadband sensors raising the active station count from approximately 40 to
over 100 instruments.

\subsection*{Modern High-Density Multi-Agency Architecture (2020--2025)}
The modern operational configuration represents a mature, high-density
seismological and accelerometric array combining 18 regional and international
monitoring institutions across Northeastern Italy, Austria, and Slovenia.
Active daily telemetry continuously exceeds 140 stations from 2020 to 2023,
stepping up to 266 stations during the 2024 Socchieve seismic sequence and
sustaining peak density through 2025.
